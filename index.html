Inspect a Top Hat connection

  1. Choose the roof member. In Wind & bracing → Shed generator, open Roof purlins / wall girts in the Members settings. Choose TH64100 for the first and last bays.
  2. Calculate and explore. Click Calculate shed, switch to Physical model and select roof purlin SH-027 in the first bay.
  3. Read the fixing detail. In Connections, inspect SH-027/start. Two No.12 × 20 mm screws, one per foot, resist 5.32 kN against 3.33 kN of outward-wind uplift. The purlin itself exceeds its member-table limits in this example.

Read wind pressures across a shed

  1. Set the wind case. The sample uses a 12 × 8 m gable building in region B1 with wind from the west and a 20° roof pitch.
  2. Compare the surfaces. Switch between the roof plan and the wall elevation. Trace the corner, edge and field zones back to the selected face.
  3. Check the inputs in the report. The example records pressure coefficients, terrain and opening assumptions. It gives an ultimate wind speed of 47.31 m/s for this case.

Trace a beam from a drawing

  1. Calibrate the drawing. Set the scale from a known length. Mark the ends of the 4.00 m concrete beam CB5 between L6 and L2.
  2. Edit the beam. Set the section to 250 × 500 mm, enter the 6 kN/m imposed line load and save the project.
  3. Reopen and export. The report gives 53.04 kNm at midspan. Service deflection is 9.531 mm, above the 1.000 mm limit entered for this example.

Look inside a lintel

Rotate a 2.50 m example. The steel frame has C90 chords, five alternating diagonal webs between the chords, two end posts and two 1.15 mm face plates. The full-length plates are translucent in the inspection view so the diagonal webs remain visible. Rotate the example, then compare steel without webs and solid 90 × 300 mm timber.

The limited demo uses fixed geometry. The calculator in development lets the engineer change the section, loads and restraints.
